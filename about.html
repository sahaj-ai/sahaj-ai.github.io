সহজ-Sahaj is a typing assistant for Assamese — built for the people who write in it every day: lawyers, teachers, students, writers, and anyone who's ever wished they could type Assamese as easily as English.

Made in Assam, India · v3.0

Why Sahaj exists

For years, typing Assamese on a computer meant choosing between bad options. Browser-based tools were slow and unreliable. Old desktop apps required Java — an awkward runtime that added hundreds of megabytes for no real reason. And memorising the InScript layout felt like learning a new alphabet all over again.

I was writing in Assamese myself — legal documents, letters, personal notes — and I kept wishing the experience felt as smooth as typing in English. I wanted a proper editor, spell-check, translation, and most of all, something that worked without internet. Nothing on the market came close.

So I decided to build it. Sahaj started as a small weekend project and slowly grew into a complete typing toolkit — every feature added because I actually needed it, or because a user wrote to me and asked for it.

"I built Sahaj because I needed it myself — and because I wanted Assamese typing to feel as natural as typing in English."

What Sahaj tries to be

Four principles have guided every decision in the app — from the very first line of code to the most recent update.

📴

Offline first

Most of Sahaj never touches the internet. Voice, phonetic, mouse, InScript, spell-check, and dictionary — all work without a connection, at full speed.

⌨️

Four ways to type

Because not everyone types the same way. Voice for speed, phonetic for English habits, mouse for beginners, InScript for seasoned typists. Pick whichever fits the moment.

💛

One price, no subscription

Pay once, use forever. No recurring charges, no cloud accounts, no telemetry, no ads. Just a tool that does its job.

👤

Made by one person

Every fix, every popup, every keyboard shortcut was written, tested, and obsessed over by me. There's no big company behind Sahaj — just a solo developer who cares.

Under the hood

Sahaj is built with Python and PySide6 (Qt for Python). It runs on PyTorch, ONNX Runtime, and the wonderful AI models released by AI4Bharat — specifically their voice recognition model and their transliteration engine. Hugging Face libraries handle model loading, FFmpeg processes audio, and PyInstaller packages the whole thing into a double-clickable app.

  • 🐍 Python — Core language
  • 🖼️ PySide6 / Qt 6 — GUI
  • 🔥 PyTorch — Deep learning
  • 🎙️ AI4Bharat ASR — Offline voice
  • 🔤 AI4Bharat Xlit — Offline transliteration
  • 🤗 Hugging Face — Model loading
  • ⚙️ ONNX Runtime — Inference
  • 🎬 FFmpeg — Audio decoding
  • 📦 PyInstaller — Packaging

My deep gratitude to every one of these projects and their maintainers. Full attributions are in the app's License Agreement, and on the homepage's Credits section.

A note on the online features

Two features in Sahaj reach out to the internet: Live AI transliteration, which uses Google's public Input Tools API, and the translation boxes, which use MyMemory. Both are free public services — Sahaj has no affiliation with them, and they are not part of what your license fee covers. Your ₹499 pays for the app itself and its offline features. Translation is still in Beta, so please treat its output as a helpful draft rather than a final answer. Every other feature of Sahaj works fully offline.

Where Sahaj is going

Sahaj is still growing. Future updates will focus on improving translation quality, expanding the built-in dictionary, and making voice recognition even faster. If there's a feature you'd like to see, please write to me — the best ideas so far have come from users who took a minute to send a message.

Sahaj currently runs on Windows 10/11 and Ubuntu. Both installers are included with your license — no extra cost, no separate purchase.
